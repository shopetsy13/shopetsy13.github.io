Marla to square feet and kanal to square feet calculator

Land in Pakistan and northern India is often listed in marla and kanal rather than square feet. One marla is commonly taken as 272.25 square feet, which is a square of 16.5 feet on each side. One kanal is 20 marla, which gives 5,445 square feet. These are the standard values used on this site.

Formula: square feet = marla × 272.25, and square feet = kanal × 5,445. For example, 5 marla equals 5 × 272.25 = 1,361.25 square feet, and 2 kanal equals 2 × 5,445 = 10,890 square feet.

Be careful with housing schemes. Some schemes use a smaller marla of 225 square feet, so one kanal becomes 4,500 square feet. The land area calculator lets you pick either size before you convert. Always check the size written in your property papers.

Zakat calculator: how much Zakat is due

Zakat is an annual obligation for eligible Muslims who hold wealth above the nisab threshold. The standard rate is 2.5% of net zakatable wealth. Net wealth means your eligible assets minus the debts that are due now.

The nisab is commonly measured as 612.36 grams of silver or 87.48 grams of gold. Multiply that weight by the current price per gram to get the threshold in money. With silver at 250 per gram, the silver nisab is 612.36 × 250 = 153,090. Scholars differ on which nisab to use, so check with a qualified local scholar.

Worked example: net wealth 1,000,000 is above a silver nisab of 153,090, so Zakat = 1,000,000 × 2.5% = 25,000. If net wealth is 150,000, it is below the same nisab, so no Zakat is due this year.

GST calculator: add or remove tax

A GST calculator does two jobs. It adds tax to a price that does not include tax, or it removes tax from a total that already includes it. Both work from the same rate.

To add GST: tax = price × rate ÷ 100, and total = price + tax. To remove GST: price = total ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100), and tax = total − price. For example, 1,000 with 18% added gives tax of 180 and a total of 1,180. A total of 1,180 with 18% removed gives a price of 1,000.

Common mistake: taking 18% off a total by subtracting 18% of the total. That gives the wrong price, because the tax was originally calculated on the price, not on the total.

EMI calculator for home and personal loans

EMI is the fixed monthly payment that repays a loan over its tenure. It combines principal and interest. The formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of months.

Example: a loan of 100,000 at 12% a year for 1 year gives a monthly EMI of about 8,884.88, a total payment of about 106,618.55, and total interest of about 6,618.55. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but usually increases the total interest.

Gold value calculator: tola, gram and karat

Gold in South Asia is often sold by the tola. One tola is about 11.6638 grams, and one tola equals 12 masha. The value of gold depends on the weight, the purity (karat) and the price per tola.

Formula: value = weight in tola × price per tola × karat ÷ 24. For example, 1 tola of 22K gold at 200,000 per tola is worth about 183,333. A shop price will also include making charges and its own buying or selling margin, so the result is an estimate.

BMI calculator

Body Mass Index (BMI) is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. For a person of 70 kg and 175 cm, BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9, which is in the normal range for adults. BMI does not measure body fat directly, so it is a screening measure rather than a diagnosis.

How to choose the right calculator

Many visitors arrive with one question in mind, such as how much Zakat they owe or how many marla are in a kanal. Start with the category that matches your question. Land questions go to the land area calculator, money and tax questions go to the Zakat, GST, EMI or gold calculators, and health questions go to the BMI, calorie or water calculators. If you are not sure which one fits, the simple calculator and the unit converter cover basic arithmetic and conversions for many everyday cases.

Whatever you use, check the inputs before you trust the result. A single wrong digit, such as typing 17,500 instead of 1,750, changes the answer by a factor of ten. Read the result back against your own rough estimate. If the two differ by a lot, look again at the numbers you entered and the units you selected.

Land measurement in practice: buying, selling and reading plot listings

When you read a property listing, the plot size may appear as 5 marla, 1 kanal, 10 marla or 2 kanal. Converting these into square feet makes it easier to compare plots from different areas. Divide the price by the total square feet to get a price per square foot, and compare that figure rather than the price per marla, because the marla size is not the same in every scheme.

Some common checks people make before buying land are these. How many square feet is 5 marla? It is 1,361.25 square feet at the standard 272.25. How many kanal is 10,890 square feet? It is 2 kanal, because one kanal is 5,445 square feet. How many square yards is 1 kanal? It is 605 square yards, because 5,445 ÷ 9 = 605. The land area calculator performs all of these conversions and lets you switch the marla size to 225 square feet when your scheme uses it.

Area is measured in the same way as length, and a small rounding difference can add up on a large plot. Keep at least two decimal places when you write down intermediate figures, and round only the final answer.

Zakat questions that come up most often

Zakat is one of the most searched financial topics among Muslims, and a few questions come up again and again. The first is whether jewellery is zakatable. Views differ between schools of thought and between families, so ask a scholar you trust before you calculate. The second is whether a home or car used for daily life is zakatable. In general, assets held for personal use are treated differently from assets held for trade or investment.

The third question is about the timing. Zakat is normally due once a lunar year, on wealth that has been held above the nisab for the whole year. This is why the Zakat calculator asks for the wealth you hold at the time of calculation, and why a fall below the nisab during the year is treated differently. The fourth question is about debts. Only debts that are due now are usually deducted, not long-term obligations that are paid over many years, although this too can be a matter of scholarly opinion.

Finally, people ask whether they should use the gold nisab or the silver nisab. The silver nisab is lower, so more people reach the threshold with it. Many people choose it for that reason, but the choice should follow the guidance you accept. The calculator lets you enter either metal price, so you can see both results.

GST and tax tips for sellers and shoppers

If you sell goods or services, the most useful question is whether your prices already include tax. Shops often display a single price that includes GST, and invoices may show the base price and the tax separately. To check a shop price, use the remove tax option. To price your own goods before tax, use the add tax option. Keeping the same rate on both sides of the calculation avoids the most common mistake.

Rates can change, and some goods and services fall under different rates or exemptions. Use the rate that applies to your product on the date of sale, and confirm it with your tax adviser or the official tax authority in your country. The GST calculator works with whatever rate you enter, so the result is only as correct as the rate you choose.

Loans and EMI planning

Before you take a loan, it helps to compare two or three tenures. Use the EMI calculator to see how the monthly payment falls as the tenure gets longer, and how the total interest rises at the same time. A lower EMI can make a loan affordable, but it often costs more in the end. Compare the total payment as well as the monthly figure.

The interest rate has a large effect on the result. A change of one or two percentage points can change the EMI by a noticeable amount on a large loan. When you compare offers, use the same loan amount and tenure, and only change the rate, so that you are comparing like with like. Ask the lender whether the rate is fixed or floating, because the EMI on a floating rate can change over time.

The EMI shown by a calculator is an estimate of the repayment on a standard reducing-balance loan. Lenders can add processing fees, insurance and other charges, and their schedules may differ slightly from the formula. Always ask for the official repayment schedule before you sign.

Gold buying and selling: what the price per tola means

Gold prices are often quoted per tola, but jewellers sometimes quote per gram, and dealers may use their own karat conversion. The gold value calculator converts between tola and gram, and applies the karat as a share of pure gold (karat ÷ 24). For example, 22K gold is 22 parts pure gold out of 24, which is about 91.7 per cent purity.

When you sell gold, the dealer usually pays less than the market price, and the buying rate can differ from the selling rate. Making charges on jewellery are also not part of the metal value. This is why the calculator gives an estimate of the metal value only. Use it to understand the rough worth of your gold, and then get a quote from a reputable dealer.

Health calculators: what they can and cannot tell you

BMI, calorie and water calculators are useful starting points, but they are not a full health assessment. BMI does not separate muscle from fat, so a strong athlete can have a high BMI. The calorie estimate depends on the activity level you choose, and most people overestimate how active they are. The water estimate uses body weight alone, while real needs also depend on climate, sweating, illness and diet.

If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, take medicines, or want to lose or gain weight significantly, speak with a doctor or a registered dietitian. Use the calculators to learn the numbers, and use professional advice to decide what to do with them.

Study tips for maths and formulas

When you revise formulas, it helps to know what each symbol means and where the formula comes from. For percentages, remember that the percentage is always divided by 100 before it is multiplied. For loans, the monthly rate is the annual rate divided by 12 and then by 100. For area, one kanal equals 20 marla. Writing these short rules on a card helps you check your work quickly.

The formulas library explains each formula in your language, and the solver lets you test it with your own numbers. Try solving a problem by hand first, then use the calculator to check your answer. This two-step method is one of the fastest ways to build confidence with maths.

Writing tips: counting words and characters

Many exams, job applications and social posts set limits on words or characters. Paste your text into the word counter to see the count update as you edit. Remember that different platforms count characters in different ways, especially with emojis, spaces and punctuation. If a limit is strict, leave a small margin, so that a last-minute change does not push you over.
