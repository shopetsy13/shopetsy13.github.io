Zakat Guide: How to Calculate Zakat Step by Step

This guide explains what Zakat is, how the nisab threshold works, and how to estimate your 2.5% Zakat with worked examples. You can use the Zakat calculator to do the arithmetic.

What is Zakat?

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam. It is an obligatory yearly contribution for eligible Muslims who hold wealth above a set threshold, called nisab. The money is meant to support people in need. Zakat is not the same as voluntary charity (sadaqah), although both are encouraged.

Step 1: Work out your zakatable wealth

Add up the wealth that is normally counted for Zakat, such as cash in hand and in bank accounts, savings, gold and silver you own, and business stock held for sale. Items you use personally, such as your home, your everyday clothes and your car for daily use, are usually not counted. Practices can differ, so confirm the list with a scholar if you are unsure.

Step 2: Deduct eligible debts

Subtract debts that are due now, such as bills or loans that must be repaid in the near term. The result is your net zakatable wealth. If your debts are equal to or greater than your wealth, the net amount is zero.

Step 3: Check the nisab

The nisab is commonly measured as 612.36 grams of silver or 87.48 grams of gold. To get the threshold in money, multiply the weight by the current price per gram of the metal you chose. Silver gives a lower threshold, so more people become eligible with it. Zakat is generally due when your net wealth reaches nisab and has been held for one lunar year.

Step 4: Calculate 2.5%

If your net zakatable wealth is at or above nisab, Zakat is 2.5% of that net amount. For example, if the net amount is 1,000,000, Zakat is 1,000,000 × 2.5% = 25,000.

Worked examples

  • Example 1: wealth 1,000,000, debts 0, silver price 250 per gram. Nisab = 612.36 × 250 = 153,090. Net wealth is above nisab, so Zakat = 25,000.
  • Example 2: wealth 200,000, debts 50,000, silver price 250 per gram. Net wealth = 150,000. Nisab = 153,090. Net wealth is below nisab, so no Zakat is due this year.
  • Example 3: wealth 2,000,000, debts 0, gold price 20,000 per gram. Nisab = 87.48 × 20,000 = 1,749,600. Net wealth is above nisab, so Zakat = 50,000.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the price of gold or silver from an old date. Prices change, so use a current price from a reliable source.
  • Forgetting to deduct debts that are due now.
  • Mixing up Zakat with other charity. Zakat has a fixed rate and a fixed threshold.

Frequently asked questions

What is Zakat? Zakat is an obligatory annual charity in Islam for eligible Muslims who hold wealth above a set threshold called nisab.

What is the Zakat rate? The standard rate is 2.5% of your net zakatable wealth, meaning wealth after deducting eligible debts.

What is nisab? Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that makes Zakat due. It is commonly measured as 612.36 grams of silver or 87.48 grams of gold, multiplied by the current price per gram.

Which nisab should I use, silver or gold? Scholars and communities differ on which nisab to use. Many people use the silver nisab because it is lower. Check with a qualified local scholar for your situation.

Is this calculator a religious ruling? No. The calculator gives an estimate using the standard rate and the nisab you choose. For a religious ruling, consult a qualified scholar.
